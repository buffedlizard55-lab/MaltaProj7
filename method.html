Method, sources, and findings

A reproducible snapshot of publicly advertised San Francisco city events.

Publish rule

  1. Each active row needs a title, source-stated date/cadence, San Francisco city location, organizer, and HTTPS source link.
  2. Accepted sources are official organizers, official venues, government sources, and clearly labeled trusted civic sources. The source type is visible in every row.
  3. Optional time and admission fields are not inferred. An em dash means the captured source did not state that optional value. Free and ticketed admission values are clearly highlighted with verified cost labels.
  4. Multi-city programs, cancelled events, outside-city events, and records missing essential evidence are not added to public tables. They stay on Flagged for Review with a source and explanation.

Findings, bounded by this dataset

  • The audit has 95 recurring and 45 dated-special verified rows across 8 categories.
  • The recurring list breaks down as 13 weekly, 3 monthly, 9 seasonal, 70 annual. The classification is based on cadence, not whether an event is free or public.
  • The largest audited categories are Community & culture (37), Professional & academic (26), Music & performing arts (23). This describes the verified source set, not all activity in San Francisco.
  • Organization by date prioritizes upcoming dates (from October 18, 2026 onwards) and ongoing active seasons over past 2026 editions.
  • Specialized focus highlights free admission across Gaming, Music, and Artificial Intelligence disciplines.
  • The review register makes scope and cancellation decisions visible instead of deleting difficult records or asserting unsupported city-only coverage.

Directories and coverage boundaries

Some San Francisco programming is published as a running calendar rather than a single event with a fixed cadence. Those are linked here as directories instead of forcing them into event rows:

  • San Francisco Public Library — Upcoming Events: branch programs and talks across the city system.
  • San Francisco City Guides: free volunteer-led walking tours (70+ tours, a program of SFPL); individual tours run on varying schedules via their tour finder.
  • SF Recreation and Park calendar: park programs and seasonal city events; individual dated listings (for example Scaregrove) become special-event rows.

Boundaries applied in this snapshot: recurring rows are created only when an organizer, venue, or government page states a fixed cadence (weekly, monthly, seasonal, or annual). Candidates seen only in secondary coverage or in roundup articles — for example the Bhangra & Beats Night Market, the San Francisco Hip-Hop Dance Festival, and Renegade Craft Fair mentioned on SF Travel month pages — are not listed until directly verified. Well-known look-alikes outside the City and County are excluded: the Great Dickens Christmas Fair is at the Cow Palace in Daly City, and airport programs at SFO sit in San Mateo County.

Line-by-line verification

On October 18, 2026 every published and review row, and every field-level flag, was re-checked one by one against its linked official source pages. Values not stated by a source were blanked and flagged rather than inferred; dead or moved official links were re-pointed to live pages; and field-level flags record cross-source discrepancies. Complete batch-by-batch audit records are preserved in research/reverification-2088b727.md and research/review-813e597d.md.

Audit trail and maintenance

The complete row-level research matrix is in research/official-source-audit.md. The machine-readable source of truth is data/events.json; data/events.csv is an export. Before changing a row, re-check the official source and update the review reason when evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

This site is intentionally not an exhaustive claim: private, newly announced, or unlisted events can exist outside this dated source audit.

Scope: Publicly advertised events and recurring public programs located within the City and County of San Francisco. This is a dated, reproducible catalog; it does not claim to enumerate every private, newly announced, or unlisted event.

An em dash means the linked source did not state that optional field in the captured record. Rows needing a decision, scope correction, or an essential missing value are separated on the Flagged for Review page. Read the method and findings or the row-level source audit.
