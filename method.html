Method, sources, and findings

A reproducible snapshot of publicly advertised San Francisco city events, organised free-first.

How the site is organised

  1. Free events are the main section. Free events (main) contains only rows whose linked official source states the event is free or free for part of the programme.
  2. Paid events are a separate section. Paid & ticketed collects and organises the priced, ticketed, registration-only and unpriced rows so they never mix into the free list.
  3. Focus areas come first. Inside the free list the order is gaming, then music and performing arts, then AI and technology, then everything else — the ordering the brief asks for. Focus areas are derived from each row's verified category and its own name, never from hand-written prose, and the Focus control filters any list by them.
  4. Regular patterns are separated from dated specials. Regularly occurring holds weekly, monthly, seasonal and annual programs; Special events holds dated one-offs. Each of those pages, and every category page, shows its free rows first and its non-free rows in a second table.
  5. Everything is also available in one date-ordered list on Upcoming by date and All events.

Publish rule

  1. Each active row needs a title, source-stated date/cadence, San Francisco city location, organizer, and HTTPS source link.
  2. Accepted sources are official organizers, official venues, government sources, and clearly labeled trusted civic sources. The source type is visible in every row.
  3. Optional time and admission fields are not inferred. An em dash means the captured source did not state that optional value; “Not stated” in the Cost column means no price was published.
  4. Multi-city programs, cancelled events, outside-city events, and records missing essential evidence are not added to public tables. They stay on Flagged for Review with a source and explanation.

How date ordering and the executive summary work

  • Every list is generated with upcoming rows first, ordered by start date, with completed editions pushed to the bottom and labelled Completed.
  • A small script re-computes each row's status against the visitor's actual current date, so the "Upcoming" / "Completed" / "Ongoing now" badge and the "in N days" label stay correct after the static build date. The same script refreshes every relative label on the dashboard's lists.
  • The dashboard's executive summary is free-first: it shows the free events closest to today, how many free events start in the next 7, 30 and 90 days, how many free programs are running now, and then — in its own clearly labelled panel — the nearest paid, registration-only or unpriced events. A build-time list is included for readers without JavaScript.

How cost is labelled

Cost classes are derived only from the admission text that the linked official page stated. Nothing is inferred from an event's type.

Free the source states no charge Free or partly free some programmes free, others ticketed Paid / ticketed the source states a price, pass or ticket Registration only the source names registration or an invitation but publishes no price Not stated the source published no price — never treated as free

Current split: 45 free, 12 free or partly free, 13 paid / ticketed, 4 registration only, 77 cost not stated. Only the free and partly-free classes appear in the main list. The rows with an unstated cost are listed, with their source links, on the Flagged for Review page and in the separate Paid & ticketed section.

Registration wording is not a price. Where a source says only “Registration required” or “By invitation only”, the row is labelled Registration only — no price published rather than “Paid”, because asserting a price would be an inference. Four rows are affected and they are recorded in flag-registration-only-cost-class.

Findings, bounded by this dataset

  • The audit has 99 recurring and 52 dated-special verified rows across 9 categories; 57 of the 151 published rows are still upcoming as of 2026-10-18.
  • Free coverage: 57 rows are free or partly free, and 12 of those are still upcoming. 77 rows have no published price, so they cannot be counted as free even where attendance may in fact cost nothing.
  • The recurring list breaks down as 14 weekly, 3 monthly, 10 seasonal, 72 annual. The classification is based on cadence, not whether an event is free or public.
  • The largest audited categories are Community & culture (39), Music & performing arts (23), Fairs & nightlife (20). This describes the verified source set, not all activity in San Francisco.
  • Most 2026 editions in this snapshot had already taken place by the October 18, 2026 check date, while several rows document confirmed 2027 editions. Rows record the audited edition, not a promise that the same dates repeat.
  • Focus-area gaps are published rather than papered over. As of October 18, 2026: no upcoming in-person free gaming event inside the city could be verified from an official source (flag-free-gaming-coverage-gap); none of the 16 AI & technology rows carries a source-stated free admission (flag-free-ai-coverage-gap); and 2027 dates for Hardly Strictly Bluegrass are published only by secondary aggregators (flag-hsb-2027-dates-unconfirmed).
  • The review register makes scope and cancellation decisions visible instead of deleting difficult records or asserting unsupported city-only coverage.

Directories and coverage boundaries

Some San Francisco programming is published as a running calendar rather than a single event with a fixed cadence. Those are linked here as directories instead of forcing them into event rows:

  • San Francisco Public Library — Upcoming Events: branch programs and talks across the city system.
  • San Francisco City Guides: free volunteer-led walking tours (70+ tours, a program of SFPL); individual tours run on varying schedules via their tour finder.
  • SF Recreation and Park calendar: park programs and seasonal city events; individual dated listings (for example Scaregrove) become special-event rows.
  • SF.gov citywide meetings and events: the city's own calendar, including free civic programs; dated listings become rows when a city page states the date and location.

Boundaries applied in this snapshot: recurring rows are created only when an organizer, venue, or government page states a fixed cadence (weekly, monthly, seasonal, or annual). Candidates seen only in secondary coverage or in roundup articles are not listed until directly verified. Well-known look-alikes outside the City and County are excluded: the Great Dickens Christmas Fair is at the Cow Palace in Daly City, and airport programs at SFO sit in San Mateo County.

Line-by-line verification

Every published and review row is checked against its linked official source page, and each session's pass is logged row by row with quoted source text: research/reverification-3977d9cb.md (this pass), research/review-3977d9cb.md (repository review and irregularities), research/verification-e79e0de2.md, research/reverification-2088b727.md, research/verification-e969f4df.md and research/reverification-55c58947.md. Values not stated by a source were blanked and flagged rather than inferred; dead or moved official links were re-pointed to live pages.

Audit trail and maintenance

The complete row-level research matrix is in research/official-source-audit.md. The machine-readable source of truth is data/events.json; data/events.csv is an export. Before changing a row, re-check the official source and update the review reason when evidence is incomplete or conflicting.

This site is intentionally not an exhaustive claim: private, newly announced, or unlisted events can exist outside this dated source audit.

Scope: Publicly advertised events and recurring public programs located within the City and County of San Francisco. This is a dated, reproducible catalog; it does not claim to enumerate every private, newly announced, or unlisted event.

An em dash means the linked source did not state that optional field in the captured record. Rows needing a decision, scope correction, or an essential missing value are separated on the Flagged for Review page. Read the method and findings or the row-level source audit.
